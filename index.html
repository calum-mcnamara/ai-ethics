Course Description

Unless you’ve been living under a rock, you’ll be aware of the profound impact that AI technologies are currently having on society. Increasingly, AI models are being given the authority to make decisions of great importance, without direct human oversight. This, in turn, raises various ethical issues. In this course, we will discuss such ethical issues, focusing in particular on two connected problems raised by modern AI: interpretability and alignment. In the first part of the course, we will ask what it means to “understand” an AI system, paying special attention to answers provided by the emerging science of mechanistic interpretability. In the second part of the course, we will then investigate what it means for an AI system to be “aligned” with human interests. Throughout, our discussions will be informed by classic theories from ethics, the philosophy of mind, the philosophy of science, and political philosophy. In the final weeks of the semester, students will have the opportunity to collaborate with one another on a research project. For this course, no prior background in AI, math, computer science, or philosophy (etc.) will be assumed. However, students will be expected to work hard to acquire bits of this background along the way.

Collaborative Research Project

Choose an AI system or proposed application. Explain what we can reasonably know about how it works, what it would mean for the system to be aligned, and what technical or institutional measures would justify deploying it.
